# Voice archetypes — scaffolding for the cold-start path

Loaded only when there is too little of the user's own writing to observe a voice. Each archetype is a *starting shape*, not a voice: every field below is tuned against the interview answers before it reaches the profile, and the profile records which archetype it came from with `confidence: low`.

**These are templates, not samples.** Nothing here is quoted from a real author, and nothing here belongs in the profile's Samples section — an archetype-built profile has no samples until the user publishes something. Present the two or three archetypes that fit the interview answers, not the whole list.

Every archetype below leaves the same three things to the interview: the subject matter, the audience, and the register floor and ceiling.

---

## 1. Builder log

Fits: solo developers, indie makers, anyone shipping their own thing in public.

- **Register** — first person, plain, present tense. Talks about the work, not about working.
- **Opens** — with the thing that happened: a bug, a number, a decision, a release. No throat-clearing, no topic survey.
- **Develops** — what broke, what was tried, what shipped. Specifics carry it: versions, timings, file names, the actual error.
- **Closes** — at the end of the content. Often mid-motion, on what is next or what is still unsolved.
- **Rhythm** — short-to-medium, uneven. One-line paragraphs are normal.
- **Formatting** — minimal. Code and commands verbatim; lists only for genuinely enumerable things.
- **Never** — announces enthusiasm ("excited to share"), narrates the journey, claims a benefit without the mechanism, ends on an inspirational line.

## 2. Practitioner teacher

Fits: people whose audience follows them to learn a repeatable method.

- **Register** — second person, direct, instructional but not lecturing. Assumes competence.
- **Opens** — with the reader's situation or the mistake most people make. Sometimes with the result the method produces.
- **Develops** — one method per piece, in the order someone would actually do it. Each step has a check that proves it worked.
- **Closes** — with the condition under which the advice does not apply.
- **Rhythm** — steady, medium sentences; the steps break the rhythm, not the prose.
- **Formatting** — numbered steps where the order matters, prose everywhere else. No bold-lead bullet stacks.
- **Never** — teaches a method the author has not run, pads a three-step process to seven, promises transformation.

## 3. Contrarian analyst

Fits: people with a position their field disagrees with, who can defend it.

- **Register** — declarative, first person, argumentative without heat.
- **Opens** — by naming the consensus in one sentence, then breaking it.
- **Develops** — the strongest version of the opposing case first, then the evidence against it. Concedes what is true in it.
- **Closes** — on what changes if the position holds, or on the condition that would prove it wrong.
- **Rhythm** — long argumentative sentences alternating with short verdicts.
- **Formatting** — prose-dominant. Evidence linked, numbers with their conditions.
- **Never** — contrarian for the shape of it, strawmans the consensus, hedges the conclusion it just argued for, borrows outrage.

## 4. Field report

Fits: consultants, agencies, anyone whose material is work done for or with someone else.

- **Register** — third person about the situation, first person about the decisions. Factual, low adjective density.
- **Opens** — with the situation and its constraint: who, what state it was in, what could not change.
- **Develops** — what was done and what happened, including what did not work. Numbers with their conditions attached.
- **Closes** — with what transfers to a different situation and what does not.
- **Rhythm** — even, reportorial. Detail clusters where the interesting part is.
- **Formatting** — prose plus one table or number block where the data earns it.
- **Never** — anonymizes into vagueness ("a large client"), reports only successes, rounds numbers to sound clean, implies causation the work did not establish.

## 5. Dry technical minimalist

Fits: engineers and maintainers whose credibility is precision, and who dislike selling.

- **Register** — terse, impersonal by default, first person only where a judgment is being made.
- **Opens** — with the fact. Often the first sentence is the whole point.
- **Develops** — only what the reader needs; the rest is linked.
- **Closes** — by stopping.
- **Rhythm** — short. Fragments allowed. No paragraph exceeds a few lines.
- **Formatting** — code, tables, exact identifiers. No emoji, no hashtags unless the platform makes them the only discovery path.
- **Never** — writes an introduction, restates the title, adds a summary of what was just said, uses an adjective where a number exists.

## 6. Community host

Fits: people whose feed is a place other people talk in — community leads, organizers, maintainers of a busy project.

- **Register** — warm, first person plural where the group is genuinely the subject, first person singular for opinions.
- **Opens** — with something the reader can respond to: an observation, a question with a real answer at stake.
- **Develops** — short, with room left for the reply. Credits people by name.
- **Closes** — on the open question, not on a call to engage.
- **Rhythm** — conversational, contractions throughout.
- **Formatting** — light; emoji only where the community itself uses them.
- **Never** — engagement bait ("thoughts?" stamped on every post), manufactured warmth, gratitude paragraphs, speaking for the group about a disagreement.

## 7. Curator

Fits: people whose value is filtering — what to read, what shipped, what matters this week.

- **Register** — brisk, opinionated in the commentary, neutral in the summary. The line between the two stays visible.
- **Opens** — with the item, not with an intro about the week.
- **Develops** — per item: what it is in one line, why it matters in one line, the author's take in one line. The take is the product.
- **Closes** — with the single item worth the reader's time if they read nothing else.
- **Rhythm** — compressed and repetitive by design; the repetition is the format, so vary the take, not the frame.
- **Formatting** — a consistent per-item shape, links inline.
- **Never** — summarizes without a take, pads the list to a round number, links something unread.

## 8. Personal essayist

Fits: people whose audience follows the person more than the topic.

- **Register** — first person, reflective, comfortable with uncertainty.
- **Opens** — inside a scene or a moment, not with the theme.
- **Develops** — the specific before the general; the general arrives late and briefly, if at all.
- **Closes** — without resolving everything. An ending that concludes reads written-to-order.
- **Rhythm** — the widest variance of these eight: long sentences beside three-word ones.
- **Formatting** — plain prose, few or no lists.
- **Never** — states the lesson the story already carried, uses a hardship as a setup for a product, ends on the takeaway.

---

## Tuning checklist

Before any archetype reaches the profile, override its fields with the interview answers, in this order: who is writing (person and number) → audience → register floor and ceiling → off-limits subjects → protected habits. Then delete every archetype line the user's answers contradicted rather than leaving it as a default nobody chose.
