# Style: ahrefs.com

Generated from 229 pages (100% of non-serial site, 134/134 pages; serial sections sampled) on 2026-08-09.

Crawled sections: product landings, free-tool landings, the blog, the `/seo/*` evergreen guides, Ahrefs Academy courses and lessons, crawler/policy docs, brand pages (about, team, tech, media kit, careers), pricing, FAQ, events/webinars, legal. Two programmatic sections — `/writing-tools/*` and `/agencies/*` — were sampled and are **excluded as anti-models** (see Genre notes).

---

## Voice profile

The author is a working marketer who happens to own the data. They talk to one reader — "you", another practitioner — as a peer who is competent but busy, and they never perform expertise. Authority comes from two things and nothing else: exact numbers from Ahrefs' own index or studies, and a visible willingness to say what the product cannot do. Where a normal SaaS page reaches for an adjective, this author reaches for a figure; where a normal page hides a limitation, this author puts it in a heading.

The register is business-casual and contraction-heavy, closer to a good internal Slack explanation than to marketing copy. Sentences are short. Paragraphs are one to three sentences. Enthusiasm is rationed and usually outsourced — to a named employee's quote, or to a customer testimonial left unedited with its typos intact. Humor exists but is dry, one beat long, and never costs the reader a fact.

The most distinctive habit is self-undercutting: assert something confidently, then immediately qualify it, correct a likely misreading, or name the caveat. "That's not necessarily true, since…" is as characteristic of this voice as any product noun.

## Tone rules

**Do**

- Address the reader as "you" and make them the grammatical subject of benefit lines.
- Use "we/our" for Ahrefs the company: its crawlers, its methodology, its decisions, its limits.
- Attach a number to every claim — unrounded, with its date or sample size when it's from a study.
- State a limitation right after the claim it qualifies. End methodology sections with the limits item.
- Answer a question with the verdict word first: "No." / "Yes — free and no signup." / "Two ways." / "It depends."
- Name competitors and third-party tools flatly, including where they beat Ahrefs.
- License a superlative with an attribution or a count: "the #1 most active SEO crawler (according to Cloudflare Radar)", "the largest index of 35.0 trillion".
- Use contractions everywhere, including in docs and policy pages.
- Open sentences with But, So, And, Now, Well, Plus, Unfortunately.
- Put the concrete example immediately after the abstract rule: "For example," + a real domain, a real keyword in curly quotes, a real figure.
- Remove friction out loud: "No sign-up required, and absolutely free of charge", "No code required", "in hours, not weeks".

**Don't**

- Don't assert a benefit without a number, a named report, or a study link behind it.
- Don't use a superlative about Ahrefs in Ahrefs' own voice unless a number backs it.
- Don't hide a caveat, and never bury it below the fold.
- Don't write a paragraph over four sentences, and never stack two long sentences.
- Don't decorate: no emoji bullets, no exclamation stacking, no "Let's explore the exciting world of…".
- Don't hedge with corporate softeners ("perhaps", "arguably", "it's important to note", "at the end of the day").
- Don't say "solution" as a euphemism for a product, or "leverage"/"utilize" where "use" works.
- Don't let a joke replace an explanation.
- Don't write "I" on a page with no byline.

## Lexicon

**Product nouns.** Title Case, used as bare nouns without "the": Site Explorer, Keywords Explorer, Site Audit, Rank Tracker, Content Explorer, Brand Radar, Bot Analytics, GSC Insights, Content Gap, Link Intersect, Page Explorer, Link Explorer, Structure Explorer, Portfolios, Patches, Alerts, Batch Analysis, Report Builder, Agent A, Ahrefs MCP, AhrefsBot, Ahrefs Free, Letaido, Firehose, Yep. Reports are named in-app style plus the word "report": "the Matching terms report", "the Organic competitors report". Navigation is written as a path: "Site Explorer > Top Pages".

**Metrics as proper nouns**, acronym in parentheses on first use: Domain Rating (DR), URL Rating (UR), Keyword Difficulty (KD), Share of Voice (SOV), Parent Topic, Traffic Potential, Traffic Value, Health Score, Ahrefs Rank.

**Possessive brand form** with a curly apostrophe is the house modifier: "Ahrefs' proprietary web index", "Ahrefs' Keywords Explorer", "Ahrefs' blog". Never "the Ahrefs index".

**Verb bank** — headings and bullets start here: find, see, track, monitor, discover, uncover, spot, catch, check, surface, benchmark, diagnose, fix, audit, reverse-engineer, spy on, automate, localize, claim, reclaim, plug X into, navigate to, set the filter, export.

**Value adjectives, used sparingly:** actionable, practical, untapped, low-competition, high-volume, hidden, in-depth, easy-to-digest, no-nonsense, free.

**Friction-removal formulas:** "No sign-up required, and absolutely free of charge" · "No code required" · "no developers needed" · "in minutes instead of hours" · "in hours, not weeks" · "no context switching" · "You don't have to fire up a crawler, spam your server, and wait for the data."

**Fixed furniture that recurs verbatim** across unrelated pages — reuse these rather than inventing variants:

- "Below are some actionable ways to use this report." / "Here are some actionable use cases for this report."
- "Questions? We have answers" (FAQ heading)
- "Have other questions? If you have more questions, contact us so we can help."
- "Get started. Pick a plan that suits your needs, and we'll see you on the other side."
- "Or get an Ahrefs Free account →"
- "More reasons to use Ahrefs as your go-to marketing platform"
- "Looking for more resources? We've got you covered!"
- "KEEP LEARNING" · "What you'll learn" · "About this course" · "Course by" · "Final thoughts" · "Learn more →"
- The live counter, unformatted with no thousands separator: "18291 users joined Ahrefs in the last 7 days"

**Numbers are the vocabulary.** Digits always, even for small values. Precision over rounding: 90.63%, 61.6%, 2.15 days, 35.0 trillion, 28.7 billion, 18291. A `+` suffix marks an open-ended count (250+, 190+, 475M+). Magnitudes are abbreviated (7.8k, 475M+, 320PB, $4.2M).

**Words this author never uses** (verified absent from Ahrefs' own copy across the corpus): revolutionary, cutting-edge, game-changing, game-changer, seamless(ly), synergy, holistic, paradigm, best-in-class, world-class, next-level, supercharge, effortless(ly), elevate, unlock the power of, delve, furthermore, moreover, in today's fast-paced world, journey, empower, utilize, leverage (as a verb), robust, "we're excited to announce", "solution" as a vague noun. Where these appear on the site, they are inside a customer quote, an agency-submitted blurb, or a programmatic tool page — never in first-party prose.

**Anti-hype phrases that *are* house voice:** "We don't guess — we study them at scale." · "Honest about limits" · "modeled visibility signals — potential exposure, not measured audience reach" · "Coverage is strongest in English" · "Be wary of tool creators who suggest otherwise" · "No source of traffic is truly free." · "without fluff and false promises of 'passive income.'"

## Rhythm & syntax

- **Sentences: 8–22 words.** Median around 15. A 25-word sentence reads long here. Longer sentences (35–45 words) appear only when unpacking a mechanism in a guide, and are always followed by a short one.
- **Paragraphs: 1–3 sentences.** The single-sentence paragraph is the default unit in guides, blog posts, and Academy lessons. Landing-page blocks are one or two sentences under a micro-heading.
- **Long-then-short is the cadence.** An explanatory sentence lands, then a 3–8 word verdict: "Truth is, they're doing it wrong." / "And it's simply beautiful." / "So we built it." / "This is why SEO matters."
- **Fragments are a tool, not an error** — used as answer beats and emphasis: "Keyword research." / "Constantly." / "No." / "Period." / "Well, not quite."
- **Signature move 1 — rhetorical question, blunt answer.** "So does publishing more mean ranking more? … Not really." / "More metrics, better decisions, right? Wrong."
- **Signature move 2 — assert, then undercut.** "So the faster your pages, the more of them get crawled." → "That's the oversimplified version, and the exceptions matter."
- **Signature move 3 — "X, not Y" antithesis.** "in hours, not weeks" · "Search backed prompts (not synthetic prompts)" · "no vanity rankings, just metrics that move the needle" · "Not a filtered API. Not rate-limited endpoints."
- **Signature move 4 — the colon payoff.** Long setup, blunt short resolution: "Translation: a gap someone else will fill if you don't." / "The takeaway: fix the template once, not every page."
- **Signature move 5 — "For example," plus a concrete number**, fired three to eight times per teaching page.
- **Em dash is the house punctuation**, not a thing to avoid. Usually unspaced in editorial and newer landing copy ("your website—no developers needed"), sometimes spaced on marketing pages. A spaced hyphen appears in `Label - gloss` bullet lines. En dash for ranges (15–30 minutes).
- **Parentheses carry glosses, asides, and jokes** — heavily used: "(i.e., blog articles)", "(bolding is mine)", "(or your wallet)", "(formerly Google My Business)".
- **Curly double quotes wrap every UI label and query example:** "how to lose weight", the "Also rank for" toggle, "Cluster by Parent Topic".
- **Scare quotes hold industry jargon at arm's length:** "authority", "link juice", "money pages", "zero-click search", "spying", "passive income".
- **Ellipsis marks a held beat:** "Three sprints, two rewrites, one launch... and the page never got indexed."
- **Colons introduce lists constantly**, and the stem is usually "Here's/Here are": "Here are a few best practices:".
- **Oxford comma in triples.** Semicolons essentially absent. Commas sparse.
- **Ampersand replaces "and" inside headings and tight labels** ("Plan, Publish & Optimize", "clicks & impressions") but never in body prose.
- **US spelling** throughout (optimize, analyze, behavior). Legal pages are the exception and use British-Singapore forms.

## Structure

**Product landing page**

1. Eyebrow: a breadcrumb label with a trailing slash on its own line — "All tools /", "Free SEO Tools /", "Academy /".
2. H1: benefit or capability in sentence case, often imperative — "See which bots crawl your website and stop the bad ones".
3. One-sentence subhead naming the mechanism, not the feeling.
4. Primary CTA (2–3 words, verb-first: Sign up for Free, Get Started, Start course), with a **reassurance or availability line directly under the button** — "Available on all plans while in beta." / "No sign-up required, and absolutely free of charge".
5. Feature blocks: product or feature name as a micro-heading + one to two sentences + `Learn more →`.
6. Closing line that restates the H1 promise as two imperative sentences.
7. **The live signup counter sits between the closing line and the repeated CTA.**
8. FAQ headed "Questions? We have answers"; answers open with the verdict word; closes on "Have other questions? …".
9. Cross-sell block: "More reasons to use Ahrefs as your go-to marketing platform".

**Research-led landing** (the current house template for AI-era pages): breadcrumb → H1 + two-sentence value line with friction removers → headline stat tile → "What is X?" definition including the mechanism → "Why track X?" backed by two of Ahrefs' own studies as big-number tiles with "Read the … study →" → "X vs. Y: what's the difference?" → related tools → **"How Ahrefs sources … results"** as a numbered methodology whose final item is always **"Honest about limits"** → free-report scope → upsell → pricing → FAQ.

**Blog post:** author byline with role above the fold → "Article Performance / Data from Ahrefs" widget showing the post's own metrics (dogfooding as proof) → Contents → hook framed as the reader's problem → one-line scope promise → numbered H2 sections → inline callouts (`Sidenote.`, `pro tip`, `advanced tip`) → **"Final thoughts"** (never "Conclusion") that zooms out and usually deflates → personal sign-off, not a corporate CTA: "Got more questions? Ping me on X." → byline repeated → "Keep Learning".

**Evergreen guide (`/seo/*`):** "By <Name> / <Role> at Ahrefs" plus Contributors and "Reviewed by" → chapter rail 01–10 → shocking-stat cold open → a one-word paragraph naming the topic → an explicit time budget for the reader ("about 20 minutes") → an honest "but there are caveats" → "So let's dive right in." → `Part N` sections with verb-first subheads → `Did you know?` and `Key takeaways` callouts → numbered References with retrieval dates → "Master SEO Step by Step" chapter grid.

**Academy lesson:** breadcrumb `Academy / How to Use Ahrefs / Module N` → lesson title → one-sentence appositive definition of the tool or report → the hinge line **"Below are some actionable ways to use this report."** → H2s written as the user's goal, verb-first (See / Find / Check / Figure out / Track) → `TIP` / `NOTE` callouts → `KEEP LEARNING` with three to six blog links → `NEXT <lesson>` + runtime → full module index with per-lesson `mm:ss` → "About this course" → "What you'll learn" bullets, nearly all "How to <verb> <object> with <Tool>" → instructor bios in third person → certification exam pitch.

**Docs / policy:** purpose stated with its restraint in the first paragraph ("while minimizing load on their servers") → a Contents list mirroring the H2s → spec sections as label/value pairs → third-party validation → "Benefits for site owners", which reframes the crawl as a favor → copy-pasteable directives → a close with a real mailbox and an offer to help.

**FAQ answers** open with the verdict word, then explain, then name the product only as the resolution in the last sentence.

## Formatting habits

- **Arrows are semantic and carry every secondary link:** `→` internal / next / learn-more; `↗` external or new tab. They do the decorative work emoji would do elsewhere.
- **Callout labels shift case by genre** — Academy: `TIP`, `NOTE`, `KEEP LEARNING`, `NEXT` in all caps. Blog: lowercase `pro tip`, `advanced tip`, and the distinctive `Sidenote.` with a trailing period. Guide: sentence case `Did you know?`, `Key takeaways`. Badges: `New`, `Soon`, `BEST VALUE`, `Bonus`.
- **Bold is nearly absent from body prose.** Emphasis comes from heading hierarchy, one-sentence paragraphs, and curly quotes. Where bold appears, it carries a UI label or a feature-block micro-heading.
- **Two bullet shapes.** (a) Bare parallel fragments, no terminal punctuation. (b) `Lead-in – gloss` or `Lead-in: gloss`, where the lead-in is a bolded phrase or a term. Lists run three to six items, never nested.
- **Every list is introduced by a colon-terminated stem**, usually "Here's/Here are".
- **Numbered steps are bare digits**, one short imperative each, naming the literal UI path.
- **Links are full descriptive titles**, never "click here". `Learn more →` is the one generic label allowed.
- **Stats render as big-number + label pairs**, not prose: "-58% / Clickthrough rate for the #1 result".
- **Provenance lines are explicit:** "Source: …", "according to Google", "BrightLocal's 2021 study shows that…", with dates.
- **Screenshot captions are either declarative takeaways** ("Backlinks help pages rank higher in Google's search results.") **or bare labels** ("Top pages report in Site Explorer."). Body copy hands off with a colon and lets the image carry it.
- **Emoji: effectively banned.** Roughly one per fourteen pages, and only as a badge (`TIP 💡`, `NOTE 📝`, `Offer 🎉`, `📆` on webinar dates) or a single dry wink in the About story. Never as bullets, never in headings, never in docs, guides, or product prose.
- **Testimonials are left unedited**, typos and emoticons intact. Authenticity beats polish.

## Genre notes

**Landing pages** — most templated, most fragment-driven, zero first-person singular. Enthusiasm arrives only as an attributed employee quote.

**Blog** — bylined, first-person, most personality. Opens by giving the answer away on purpose; closes on "Final thoughts" and a personal ask ("Ping me on X").

**Evergreen guides (`/seo/*`)** — the teaching voice at full length. Heaviest use of assert-then-undercut, hedge vocabulary where accuracy demands it, and cited references with retrieval dates.

**Academy lessons** — same person, slower and more procedural. Near-identical templates, one opinion per lesson, "Let's say…" scenarios, exact filter values.

**Docs / crawler policy** — the most formal register the site reaches, and it still uses contractions, "you", and a human email address at the end. Never corporate.

**Legal** — the only genuine register break: "shall/herein", British-Singapore spelling. Do not model prose on it, and do not let its formality leak into any other genre.

**Excluded as anti-models.** Two programmatic sections contradict the house voice and should never be used as reference:

- `/writing-tools/*` — machine-written blurbs full of exactly the banned vocabulary ("Elevate your writing effortlessly", "Experience the power of efficiency!", "Unlock emotions", "cutting-edge", "Seamlessly"), plus the site's only concentration of exclamation marks.
- `/agencies/*` — third-party agency copy with hype adjectives, "synergy", mixed spelling conventions, and visible template seams (a Japanese or German H1 over English body copy). Only one sentence on those pages is first-party prose: the single line introducing the agency directory.

Also note: event and webinar pages run a faster, less-edited register (inline `•` bullets, all-caps hype, duplicated words), and are the only place flag emoji appear.

## Rewrite instructions

**Preserve from the source document, unchanged:**

- Every fact, figure, date, name, product name, URL, code sample, and command. Never invent a statistic to satisfy the "attach a number" habit — if the source has no number, restructure the sentence instead of fabricating one.
- The source language. This style is defined by rhythm, lexicon, and structure; it applies in any language the source is written in.
- The logical order of the argument and the document's own section boundaries. Headings may be rephrased into this voice, but sections must not be merged, reordered, or dropped.
- Any legal, safety, or compliance wording, verbatim.

**Replace:**

- **Voice.** Convert third-person and passive constructions to second person with the reader as subject. Convert institutional "the company" to "we" where the referent is the document's own author-organization.
- **Rhythm.** Break long sentences at their natural pivot. Target 8–22 words. Split any paragraph over three sentences. After a long explanatory sentence, add or promote a short verdict sentence — but only where the source already implies that conclusion.
- **Lexicon.** Delete every banned word listed above and substitute the plain equivalent (use, not utilize; use, not leverage). Replace vague evaluative adjectives with the figure the source already contains.
- **Openings.** Replace throat-clearing preambles with either the reader's problem stated flatly, a shocking figure from the source, or a definition sentence. Never open with "In today's…".
- **Closings.** Replace "Conclusion" with "Final thoughts". End on a deflation, a caveat, or a forward-looking single line — not a summary of what was just read.
- **Formatting.** Convert emphasis-bold in body prose to sentence structure or curly quotes. Convert emoji bullets to plain bullets. Convert "click here" links to descriptive titles. Add `→` to secondary CTAs and `↗` to outbound links. Keep at most one emoji per document, as a badge, and only if the source already had one.
- **Callouts.** Map the source's asides onto the genre's convention: `TIP` / `NOTE` for instructional documents, `Sidenote.` for editorial, `Did you know?` / `Key takeaways` for long-form guides.

**How strictly to imitate:**

Imitate the synthetic samples at the level of sentence shape and paragraph length, not phrasing. Reuse the fixed furniture verbatim only when the document genuinely has that slot — an FAQ heading, a lesson hinge line, a closing CTA. Do not bolt on a stat tile, a signup counter, or a methodology block that the source document has no content for.

Two habits are non-negotiable, because without them the output reads as generic SaaS copy wearing this voice as a costume:

1. **Every claim carries its evidence** — a number, a named source, or a link that the source document already supplied.
2. **Every confident assertion that deserves a caveat gets one, immediately.** If the source document contains a limitation, a failure mode, or an "except when", surface it rather than smoothing it away. This voice buys trust by conceding.
