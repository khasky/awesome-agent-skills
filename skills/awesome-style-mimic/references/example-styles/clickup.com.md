# Style: clickup.com

Generated from 241 captured pages (295 fetches, 26 failed, remainder deduped after redirects) — 100% of non-serial URLs discovered, plus sampled sets from 23 serial patterns (blog, templates, features, compare, integrations, customers, events, partners, learn-hub topics) — on 2026-08-09.

## Voice profile

ClickUp writes as a confident operator talking to another competent operator who is short on time. The author never introduces itself, never warms up, and never hedges: it names the reader's pain in the reader's own vocabulary, states the fix flat, and moves on. The company is usually a third-person actor in its own copy ("ClickUp centralizes…", "ClickUp gets work done") while the reader owns everything in the sentence ("your team", "your Workspace", "your hours"). "We" is rationed to four jobs — origin story, promises the company is legally on the hook for, editorial method, and one sanctioned repair line ("we fixed it"). "I" belongs to customers, podcast hosts, and the CEO, never to the product.

The villain is always a *thing*, never the reader and never a named competitor: Work Sprawl, disconnected tools, silos, busywork, toggling, status meetings. The reader is absolved, the tools are blamed, and the fix is always consolidation into "one place." Humor exists — dry, one beat per page, parked at the end of a block or a CTA, always aimed at the old way of working, never at the reader.

Proof is numeric and unfootnoted: precise digits with a `+` or `%`, sometimes with a bare parenthetical source. Superlatives are asserted, not argued.

## Tone rules

**Do**

- Open on the reader's problem or a concrete scene. First sentence carries information.
- Address the reader as "you"; make "your" modify most object nouns.
- Refer to the company by name in the third person in feature and benefit copy.
- Name the enemy as a system, not a person or a rival brand.
- State claims flat. Back them with a digit, then stop.
- Use fragments as full sentences for landing a point.
- Close every block on the fix, never on the problem.
- Keep one dry joke per page, at most, and place it at the end.
- Name competitors plainly when comparing; state their limits as facts.
- Let customers say the emotional and unpolished things (typos and all).

**Don't**

- No hedging: *might*, *may help*, *could potentially*, *we believe*, *arguably*, *results may vary*.
- No formal connectives: *however*, *moreover*, *furthermore*, *therefore*, *additionally*, *in order to*. Use "Plus," / "So" / "But" / "Still".
- No consultant filler: *synergy*, *best-in-class*, *world-class*, *cutting-edge*, *revolutionary*, *game-changing*, *paradigm*, *holistic*, *ecosystem*, *delve*, *journey*, *in today's fast-paced world*, *at the end of the day*.
- No *leverage* or *utilize* as verbs (they survive only in legal prose and quotes).
- No apology or service-desk register: *unfortunately*, *please note*, *we're sorry*, *we're excited to announce*.
- No "click here", no bare URLs in marketing copy, no generic anchor text.
- No mocking a competitor in the brand's own voice — outsource the sharp line to a quoted review.
- No emoji outside the blog (see Genre notes).
- Never end on the problem; never leave a rhetorical question unanswered.

## Lexicon

**House concepts (capitalized, asserted without definition):** Work Sprawl (and AI Sprawl, App Sprawl, Context Sprawl, Knowledge Sprawl), Converged AI Workspace / convergence, the everything app for work, single source of truth, 100% context, the work graph, zombie projects, the strategy-execution gap.

**Product nouns are proper nouns mid-sentence, brand-prefixed on first use:** ClickUp Brain, Brain MAX, Super Agents, Ambient Agents, Certified Agents, AI Notetaker, Enterprise Search, Talk to Text, Docs, Chat, Dashboards, Automations, Whiteboards, Sprints, Goals, Custom Fields, ClickApps, Workspace, Space, Folder, List, Board view, Workload view. A generic word gets capitalized the moment it names a ClickUp object ("store multiple Lists in a Folder") — the single most identifiable lexical tic.

**Pain vocabulary — physical and concrete:** silos, bottlenecks, busywork, toggling, tool hopping, back-and-forth, chasing updates, scattered, fragmented, buried, scope creep, firefighting, handoffs, work about work, status meetings, spreadsheets, VLOOKUPs, PowerPoint decks, folder trees.

**Verb bank:** centralize, consolidate, unify, streamline, automate, surface, flag, route, triage, orchestrate, ship, execute, accelerate, eliminate, reclaim, free up, break down silos, keep work moving, get work done.

**Payoff adverb:** *faster* ends more sentences than any other word. *actually* is the signature qualifier ("reports you can actually work from", "AI that actually showed up to work") — it implies every competitor's version is theater.

**Fixed micro-copy, reused verbatim site-wide:** `Get started. It's FREE` · `Free forever. No credit card.` · `25,000+ reviews from` · `Trusted by the best` · `Don't just take our word for it` · `Save one day every week. Guaranteed.` · `No, I want to waste 1 day per week` (the decline link under every email capture).

**Numbers are lexicon, not evidence.** Always digits, never spelled out, `+` for floors: 25,000+ reviews, 3M+ teams, 1,000+ integrations, 15+ views, 384% ROI, 92,400 hours, 52 days back, 4x, 100x. Third-party sources are named inline or in a bare parenthetical — `(McKinsey Report)`, `according to Forrester` — never linked, never dated.

## Rhythm & syntax

- **Sentences 8–25 words**, dropping to 3–7-word fragments for percussion. Long analytic sentence followed by a short verdict is the base move: a full sentence conceding a real cost ("the import takes an afternoon and the settings run four levels deep"), then a flat three-to-five-word verdict ("That is the price.").
- **Paragraphs 1–3 sentences.** Four is rare, five essentially absent. Nothing reads as a block of text.
- **The signature sentence shape is negate-then-assert:** "Know more. Guess less." · "Not drafts. Not reminders. Scheduled work, owned by name." · "This isn't just clutter—it's a tax on every decision your team makes." · "the fix isn't another app. It's one shared system."
- **Second signature: `so` + `without`.** Brand or triad subject → unite-verb → `so` outcome → `without` friction: *"One board holds the tasks, the files, and the deadlines so the week starts aligned without a single status call."*
- **Third: the `X, not Y` closer.** "focus on strategy, not busywork" · "Code smarter, not harder" · "with data, not opinions".
- **Em dash, unspaced, is the house mark** — appended benefit, appositive definition, or punchline swerve: "—all in one place", "—no meetings required", "zombie projects—initiatives that are technically 'in flight'". Roughly one per prose page on marketing copy, two per page on solution pages.
- **Question-then-answer beat:** "The result? Fewer bottlenecks." · "Now? ClickUp Brain just handles it." · "The best part?" Never leave the question hanging.
- **Triads are the default list length**, serial comma always: "plan, track, and run"; "tools, teams, and data"; "powerful, flexible, and intuitive".
- **Negative-parallel fragments close blocks:** "No new tabs. No switching tools." · "No digging, no pinging teammates." · "No status meeting required."
- **Anaphora for codas:** three "No more…" fragments, the third one longer than the first two: "No more relays. No more guessing. No more asking three people which file is the real one."
- **Colons introduce every list and worked example.** Semicolons are effectively absent from marketing prose.
- **Exclamation marks** are rare in current copy — allowed in blog posts, migration reassurance, FAQ answers, and older feature pages; banned from heroes, CTAs, and enterprise/solution pages.

## Structure

- **Every marketing page is stacked blocks, not flowing argument.** No transitional prose between sections.
- **The repeating unit is three lines:** 1–3-word eyebrow (often ALL CAPS) naming the feature → sentence-case headline naming the *outcome*, verb-first → one or two sentences of mechanics → inline CTA.
- **Hero formula:** eyebrow → promise headline (4–9 words, often with a terminal period) → one-sentence gloss → primary CTA + `Free forever. No credit card.` → proof strip (`25,000+ reviews from` / `Trusted by the best`).
- **Before/after contrast is the most reproducible device:** two mirrored columns with matched counts and parallel grammar — `Without ClickUp X` / `With ClickUp X`, `The old way` / `The ClickUp way`, `Current State` / `Future State`, or ❌ five-and-five against ✅ five-and-five on solution pages. Left column is verb-led pains, right column noun-led relief, no terminal punctuation.
- **Closing CTA restates the H1** almost verbatim (sometimes a third time on the page), or swaps in an aphorism, and re-attaches the friction remover.
- **FAQ sits at the bottom**, questions written in the reader's own voice ("How easy is it to migrate from X?", "Can I upgrade myself?"), and on comparison pages always includes a competitor question.
- **Blog skeleton:** category → title (number + superlative + year) → byline with job title → date → "Nmin read" → four-beat intro (pain question → cost → diagnosis → "In this guide, we'll break down…") → TOC → H2 sections → listicle items in a fixed order (`X best features` / `X limitations` / `X pricing` / `X ratings and reviews`) → outro H2 that is itself a CTA phrase → imperative sign-up line with risk reversal.
- **Customer story skeleton:** `Customer Story` eyebrow → H1 "How <Company> <outcome> with ClickUp" → metric cards (big number + lowercase fragment that grammatically finishes it) → Company Overview → Industry/Employees/Use Case → Challenge/Solution/Impact (or the newer In Frame / Solution / Impact / Looking ahead) → closing quote → three-offer CTA stack.
- **Section headings are double-decked:** a plain category label above a concrete, sometimes ironic written subhead ("The Solution" over "Why another spreadsheet wouldn't do").

## Formatting habits

- **No bold or italics inside body prose.** Emphasis is carried by short lines, capitalized product nouns, ALL-CAPS eyebrows, or a single all-caps word mid-sentence ("because it IS the work", "It's FREE"). Bold appears only as a list lead-in label.
- **Bullets are unpunctuated fragments**, 3–7 items, strictly parallel (all verb-first or all noun-first). Where a bullet needs explaining it becomes `**Term**: explanation` with no terminal period.
- **Emoji are banned in product, solution, compare, customer, events, learn-hub, and legal copy** — zero across hundreds of pages. They are grammar only in the blog (see Genre notes).
- **Link text is a verb or a destination noun**, never "click here", never a raw URL (legal and trust pages are the exception and print bare URLs and email addresses).
- **Ampersand in headings and labels** ("Docs & Wikis", "Goals & OKRs"), "and" in prose. `+` sometimes substitutes in headings ("OneDrive + ClickUp").
- **`™` in page titles only**, plain "ClickUp" in body copy.
- **Numbers always as digits**, thousands separated, `+` for floors, `%` and `$` inline. Spelled-out numerals only for rhetorical effect ("Twenty-four seats. One evening. Zero slides.") or inside legal parentheticals.
- **Testimonials run raw** — name + job title (+ company), typos, caps and mild profanity preserved as an authenticity signal.
- **Tables** appear for feature comparisons and plan tiers only; competitor cells never contain a bare "No" — they carry a short disqualifying phrase ("Plugin required", "Enterprise plan limited to 50").

## Genre notes

The site is one lexicon across six separable registers. Pick one and hold it; do not blend.

**1. Product / solution marketing (default).** Fragments, ALL-CAPS eyebrows, em-dash payoffs, before/after grids, zero emoji, zero exclamation marks, one dry joke at the closing CTA. This is the register to use unless the brief says otherwise.

**2. Blog / SEO long-form.** The only register with emoji, and there they are a typed grammar, not decoration: badge prefixes at line start (📮 ClickUp Insight, 💡 Pro Tip, 🧠 Fun Fact, 👀 Did You Know?, 📖 Read More) and a semantic mini-set inside modules (✅ upside, ⚠️ downside, 🏆 best for, 📌 example), roughly one per 120 words, never two in a sentence. Exclamation marks allowed, contractions mandatory, "we" is the editorial team doing labor for the reader, an "I" practitioner voice appears when the byline has a job title. Competitor weaknesses are always *limitations*, never "cons".

**3. Comparison pages.** Headline is a two-clause antithesis split by a hard period — the rival's clause gets a passive record-keeping verb, ClickUp's clause gets a completion verb. Pain triad of 2–4-word labels, a migration-reassurance beat (a "switching?" question answered with an exclamation-mark promise that nothing must be rebuilt), then a feature table. Older pages carry puns on the competitor's name; the newer programmatic vs-page template drops all humor and moves the FAQ above the table.

**4. Customer stories.** Third-person business journalism. ClickUp never says "we". A named human carries the action (full name + title on first mention, first name after), evaluation is staged as adversarial and the win reported in one flat clause. Success is proved by what stopped happening. Long setup sentence, then a 4–9-word punch.

**5. Learn hub (editorial/analyst).** The outlier register: no contractions, no em dashes at all (the colon does that job), no exclamation marks, no emoji, no first person singular. "We" appears only for method and disclosure (a flat statement of how the tools were tested, weighted, and scored). Whole sections exist to refuse the sale — "Who Should NOT Use Jira", "When a Gantt Chart Is the Wrong Tool" — competitors get recommended by name, the ClickUp conflict of interest is restated on every commercial page, and every page ends in an FAQ whose answers open with a bare "Yes." / "No." / "It depends."

**6. Legal / trust.** First-person plural for binding promises (data-handling commitments the company is contractually on the hook for), third-person "ClickUp" for auditable claims. The Privacy Policy writes its headings in the reader's own first person ("What Choices do I have…?"). One warm opening line, then contractual. ALL-CAPS blocks for warranty and liability.

## Rewrite instructions

**Preserve from the source, unchanged:** every fact, number, date, name, product term, and technical claim; the source's language (translate nothing unless asked); the order of the underlying argument; any code, commands, and identifiers verbatim; anything the source explicitly warns about or qualifies for safety or legal reasons.

**Replace:** voice, sentence rhythm, paragraph length, lexicon, heading style, and formatting.

**Method:**

1. **Pick the register first** (Genre notes). Default to product/solution marketing. Blog register only when the source is a long-form article with a byline. Learn-hub register when the source is a neutral comparison or reference piece and must stay non-promotional. Mixing registers within one document is the single most detectable failure.
2. **Cut the warm-up.** Delete any scene-setting first sentence ("In today's…", "As organizations increasingly…"). Open on the pain, a scene, or a number.
3. **Flip the POV.** Second person for the reader; the subject of the piece named in the third person; "we" only for promises, method, origin, or the one repair line.
4. **Shorten hard.** Break every paragraph over three sentences. Break every sentence over ~28 words. Insert a 3–7-word fragment after each long sentence that lands a claim.
5. **Apply the three shapes** at least once each per section, but never twice in a row: negate-then-assert; `so`/`without` outcome clause; `X, not Y` closer.
6. **Name the enemy** as a system noun, and absolve the reader.
7. **Convert lists** to parallel unpunctuated fragments, or to `**Term**: explanation` pairs. Convert any two-sided comparison into the mirrored before/after block with matched counts.
8. **Restate the opening promise as the closing line**, then attach a concrete next step.
9. **Sweep the banned list** (Tone rules → Don't) and the hedging list. Replace every *however/moreover/therefore* with a period or "So".
10. **Strip emphasis** — no bold or italics in prose. Capitalize product nouns instead. Emoji only if you chose the blog register, and then only per the typed grammar.

**Imitation strictness:** match the synthetic samples at the level of sentence shape and paragraph length, not wording. Reuse the fixed micro-copy strings (`Get started. It's FREE`, `Free forever. No credit card.`, `25,000+ reviews from`) only when the document is genuinely ClickUp marketing; for any other subject, imitate the *pattern* — short promise plus friction remover — with the document's own words. Never invent statistics, customer names, or sources to fill a template slot: if the source has no number, drop the number slot rather than fabricate one.
