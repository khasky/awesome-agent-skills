# Style: buffer.com
Generated from 201 pages (100% of non-serial site coverage, 8 serial sections sampled) on 2026-08-09.

## Voice profile
Buffer writes as a small, real team ("we" — teammates named with full name and title, never a faceless brand) talking to one busy creator or small-business owner ("you"). The persona is a calm, radically transparent practitioner: it publishes its own salaries, revenue, and bad months, volunteers its product's limits inside sales copy, and discloses its own bias when recommending itself. It sells by removing risk and effort — everything is free-first, low-pressure, and hedged honestly — never by urgency, fear, or hype. On the blog the voice becomes a named "I": a warm, self-deprecating columnist who earns authority with credentials and data, then walks back their own headline promise before the pitch. Warmth is delivered through reassurance, parenthetical asides, and permission-giving, not jokes at anyone's expense.

## Tone rules
Do:
- Open every FAQ answer with a bare verdict, then explain: "Yes.", "No.", "Not yet.", "Absolutely!", "You sure can!", "Yes, but with limits.", "Nope — you're always the last step."
- Volunteer limitations unprompted, then offer the workaround in the next sentence: verdict first ("Not yet."), then the constraint stated plainly, then what the reader can still do today.
- Disclose bias when recommending Buffer: "I'm definitely biased here, but…", "no affiliate links, no paid placements".
- De-hype your own topic: "There's no magic, universal best time", "Buffer won't promise 'instant growth.'", "timing isn't a magic bullet".
- Hedge data claims: "tends to", "associated with", "in this dataset", "our best guess", "treat this as a first look rather than a final verdict".
- End features with a benefit tail: "…so you can show up consistently".
- Credit people by name and title: "[Full Name], our resident data scientist".
- Reassure with parentheticals: "(the final words are always yours)", "(it's free!)", "(promise!)", "(no catch)".
- Keep humor light, dry, singular — one wink per page, often a pun tied to the platform ("Pin it to win it", "bye, 1 million tabs").

Don't:
- Never use urgency or scarcity: no "limited time", "act now", "don't miss out", "buy now".
- Never use fear framing or bash competitors by name; comparisons concede the rival's strength first ("While [Competitor] excels in …").
- Never stack superlatives or claim "revolutionary", "game-changing", "cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "insane".
- Never call readers "users" in marketing copy (that word survives only in legal and data-methodology text); never call staff "employees" — they are "teammates".
- Never scold the reader; correct with permission-giving: "Can't find any patterns…? That's OK."
- Never promise virality; disclaim it ("Buffer can't guarantee virality").
- Never let AI features sound autonomous: "Buffer is not an AI tool", "you're always the last step".

## Lexicon
Preferred verbs: grow, show up (consistently), plan, schedule, publish, craft, tweak, repurpose, resonate, engage, connect, build, dig into, dive in, unpack, break down, pinpoint, surface, streamline, queue up, spark, ship, double down, lean into.

Preferred adjectives: calm, consistent, free, simple, actionable, engaging, effortless, seamless, handy, scroll-stopping, data-backed, tried-and-tested, high-performing, meaningful, authentic, lightweight, generous.

Preferred nouns and phrases: channels (never "accounts" or "networks" for connected profiles), teammates, "creators, small businesses, and marketers" (the canonical audience triad), your audience, engagement, the queue, the composer, workflow, content pillars, sweet spot, "all in one place" / "one calm place" / "one simple dashboard", "in seconds", "we've got you covered", "so you can …", "without X-ing" ("without leaving Buffer", "without losing your mind"), "No long onboarding. No complicated workflows.", "show up consistently", "what's resonating", "the algorithm" (singular, lightly personified).

Product features are capitalized proper nouns used unglossed: AI Assistant, Create space, Start Page, Insights, Takeaways, Community, Ideas, Add to Queue.

Words this author never uses: leverage, utilize, synergy, empower, unlock, supercharge, seamless-as-hype, enterprise-grade, solution(s) as product noun, cutting-edge, revolutionary, 10x, cheap, "book a demo", "click here", vague buzzwords it explicitly bans ("guru", "ninja", "rockstar", "passionate about"). "best in class" and "game-changer" survive at most once each, lowercase, inside blog prose describing someone else's work — never in Buffer's own product copy and never as self-praise.

Plain-limits vocabulary is its own register, used unspun in FAQs: "due to API limitations", "not currently available", "Buffer does not currently offer", "may limit certain features by account type or region", "Reconnecting the account typically resolves the problem."

## Rhythm & syntax
- Sentences run 10–25 words; paragraphs 1–3 sentences. Single-sentence and single-fragment paragraphs act as beats and pivots: "You need a system." / "Now, the results." / "Just ask."
- Verb triads (sometimes quads) are the house cadence: "Plan, schedule, and grow", "plan, craft, schedule, and analyze", "Analyze what works, ditch what doesn't, and keep growing".
- The spaced em-dash is the signature punctuation, carrying the payoff or the caveat: "in one calm place — so you can…", "the format tends to win on reach — but loses on saves".
- Colons launch reveals and lists: "The short version:", "Here's how:", "Enter: …".
- The question-then-answer beat drives transitions: "The problem? … The solution: …", "Our take? Timing isn't a magic bullet.", "Fresh out of ideas? Need help refining a post?"
- Mirrored antithesis is a favorite slogan shape: "pay for channels, not seats", "Less tab-switching, more creating", "confidence, not complexity", "a bio that sounds like you, not like a template".
- Contractions always (we've, you'll, don't, it's) — even in legal summaries. Sentence-initial And/But/So/Plus is normal.
- Exclamation marks are rationed: one per section at most, on warm beats, never in data sentences, never stacked.
- List vs prose splits by genre: marketing pages are stacked fragments and label+sentence blocks; blog posts are prose-forward with bullet bursts; FAQ answers are the longest continuous prose anywhere (3–5 sentence mini-guides).
- One vivid simile per long guide, no more — always an everyday image, never an exotic one. Everyday analogies teach concepts in a "Think of it as …" frame: workflows get compared to meal prep, commutes, magazine departments.

## Structure
- Landing/product template (rigid): kicker label ("Benefits", "Schedule", "Create", "Community", "FAQs") → benefit headline (3–7-word fragment, sentence case) → 1–2 sentence subhead → CTA. Then 4 benefit cards (bold micro-heading + one sentence) → labeled feature sections, each ending in the CTA → one named testimonial under "What people are saying" → "Resources" (3 article teasers with numeral-led Title Case titles) → FAQs → closing banner.
- The CTA system is fixed and must be quoted verbatim: primary button "Get started for free" (repeated 4–6 times per landing page). Site-wide closing banner: "Grow your social presence with confidence" + "No credit card needed. Free forever." Changelog closer: "Ready to grow with Buffer?" + "No credit card required. Cancel anytime." Blog closer: "Try Buffer for free" + the social-proof line "200,000+ creators, small businesses, and marketers use Buffer to grow their audiences every month" (variant: "200,000+ people like you use Buffer to build their brand on social media every month"). Secondary CTAs are two-word verb+noun: "Read the docs", "Browse templates", "Learn more". Never "Buy", "Upgrade", "Request a demo". The trial variant "Start a free 14-day trial" appears in landing FAQs only. CTAs repeat, they never escalate: the same neutral string follows every feature block, wording never intensifies down the page.
- Social proof is a boilerplate sentence with the noun swapped, and the figure itself varies by page type — "200,000+" on landing, press, free-tools and blog footers, "Join 75,000+ growing businesses…" on glossary pages. Reuse an existing variant; never invent a number.
- Landing FAQ blocks are shared boilerplate: the "Some of our favorite features include:" seven-item list is reused verbatim with only the platform name swapped, and the closing "How do I start scheduling my … posts?" answer always runs the same three moves — 14-day free trial → connect your channel → "start scheduling immediately".
- Openings are benefit-first with zero throat-clearing; the reader's problem or desire comes before the product. Blog posts open with a personal confession, aphorism, or contrarian hook; data pieces open answer-first (the finding in sentence one), then complicate it.
- Headings sell outcomes, not features: "Never miss a comment again", "Skip the guesswork", "Answers, not just analytics". FAQ questions are written in the customer's first-person voice ("Can I schedule Instagram Reels for free?"); answers are verdict-first (see Tone rules).
- Blog skeleton: hook (1–4 short paragraphs) → "Key takeaways" bullets → "Jump to a section:" TOC → question-form H2s that voice the reader's doubt → templated per-item sections → a Buffer how-to block → a closing section that walks back the hook's promise ("Timing matters — but it isn't everything") → FAQ → "More X resources" links → author bio → CTA block. Titles: numeral + benefit + year + parenthetical bonus, e.g. a how-to title of the shape "How to Plan a Month of Posts in One Afternoon (+ 12 Prompts to Get You Started)".
- Pages close by inviting, never pressuring — the last content beat de-escalates or asks the reader to reply ("Tell us @buffer. I read every reply.") before the standard banner.

## Formatting habits
- Numerals over spelled-out numbers almost everywhere: "11+ channels", "5 tips", "1080 × 1080", "8 MB". Precision is a trust signal — figures stay unrounded ("$1,910,399 (+1.21%)", "21.77%", "241,644", "$70,199.07"); marketing scale claims round upward with "+" ("200,000+", "7,000+ apps"). Dataset sizes live in titles ("New Data from 14M Posts").
- Emoji are rare, singular, functional — never decorative bullets, and essentially a `/resources/` device. They type callout boxes (💡 methodology note, ⚡ pro tip/plug, ✨ "Did you know?", ⏰ "Read more →", 📆 template tip, 📌, 📊, 👋 sign-off) and serve as ranked-list ordinals in data posts (🏅 / 🥈 / 🥉 / 4️⃣ / 5️⃣, one line per item carrying the figure and its delta). A single wink may appear in a changelog title (✨) or culture page (📚). Zero emoji in landing copy, press, shareholder letters, glossary definitions, free-tools and integrations steps.
- Bold carries structure, not emphasis: list lead-ins ("Peak posting time: …"), labels ("Best for:", "Price:", "Key takeaways"). Italic is essentially unused.
- Links are descriptive — full article titles or "Learn more about notification publishing." — never "click here" or bare URLs. Inline CTAs may end with an arrow glyph: "Sign up for free →".
- UI paths use arrows and exact label casing: "Settings → API", "click the + New button"; UI labels in quotes when ambiguous: 'click the "Create Post" button'.
- Platform naming: "X (Twitter)" on every mention; integration pairings use the multiplication sign: "Buffer × LinkedIn".
- Times as "9 a.m.", ranges "6 p.m.–11 p.m."; en-dash for all numeric ranges ("3–5 posts/week", "320–1440 px"), "×" for multipliers ("2.25×"); formulas inline in plain text: "Engagement Rate = (Total Engagements / Total Impressions) × 100".
- Acronyms are expanded on first use every time — "KPI (key performance indicator)", "return on investment (ROI)", "direct messages (DMs)". Concept definitions take a labelled inline form: "Social media marketing strategy (definition): A documented plan that…".
- Quotation marks do three jobs and no others: UI strings ('click the "Create Post" button'), pull-quotes from named experts with a said-tag, and scare quotes around the idea being debunked ("instant growth", the "dead" platforms).
- Title Case for article and tool titles; sentence case for section headings and benefit lines.

## Genre notes
Sub-profiles — only what differs from the base voice:

- **Landing/product pages**: most compressed register. Fragments and 2-sentence modules; a pun is allowed in the hero ("Pin it to win it", "Lights, camera, schedule!"); CTA after every scroll section; one joke per page maximum; no "I" anywhere. Reused stock blurbs are normal ("No long onboarding. No complicated workflows."). Feature sections carry one-word kickers (Schedule, Crosspost, Create, Plan, Community, Templates, Link-in-bio, Save and organize) over a wordplay H2; headings are transformation-framed ("Turn viewers into fans", "Turn your shorts into subscribes") or negative-promise ("Never miss a comment again"). A bridge line separates benefits from features ("One tool, endless possibilities", "Built for creators").
- **Blog/resources**: the named "I" columnist appears — personal anecdotes, self-deprecating parentheticals at the author's own expense, credential-building, bias disclosure. Furniture: Key takeaways, TOC, emoji callout boxes, comparison tables, expert quotes attributed "says [Name], [Role] at [Company]". Data studies add a warm methodology section ("how the sausage gets made") and hedge every finding; product plugs are quarantined in ⚡ callouts.
- **Glossary** (`/social-media-terms/*`): chattiest register — slang allowed ("the stuff you love", "your vibe", "win-win"), rhetorical hooks ("Ever noticed how…?"), everyday analogies. Strict template: term → one-sentence definition (repeated verbatim as the first body answer) → question-form H2s ("What is X?" → "How does X work?") → numbered steps → soft conditional plug only after the useful content ("you might enjoy… let us know what you think") → Resources.
- **Changelog**: team-diary voice with named authors. Opens directly with "You can now…" / "We've…" or by naming a long-standing friction point plainly, gives the exact click-path to find the feature, ends "← Back to changelog" + the "Ready to grow with Buffer?" banner. Exclamation marks are most at home here.
- **Shareholder letters**: epistolary and template-locked — "Hi there," → summary paragraph front-loading the numbers → "Key numbers" (MRR, ARR, MAU… each with MoM %) → reflections → "What's Coming Up" → teammates' posts by name → thank-you → signed with a dash, the sender's name, and their title ("– [Name], [Title]"). Bad news is narrated plainly, never buried. No emoji, no CTA; the only ask is "Feel free to reply."
- **Careers/culture**: warmest, most earnest register — values as creeds ("Default to transparency"), salaries published, scam warnings volunteered, a 💛 allowed. Signature anti-gatekeeping move: telling candidates outright that almost no one matches every line, and that mostly matching is reason enough to apply. Culture prose runs longer (3–5 sentence paragraphs) and swaps growth verbs for care verbs ("rest and recharge", "give back").
- **Integrations how-tos** (`/integrations/*`): near-zero humor, purely instructional. Template: app name → one-line value prop → category tag → "[App] integration for Buffer" 2-sentence intro → "Getting started" with prerequisite line ("You'll need a … account") → numbered UI-verbatim steps → honest third-party disclaimer ("This is a third-party plugin. For support, contact … directly.") → standard closing banner. Real CLI commands and JSON configs are embedded when relevant.
- **Press page**: the one page that does not sell. Boilerplate "About Buffer" paragraph, values statement, a named human contact with a real email, a label-value metrics table, two card links (transparency dashboard, fact sheet) — no persuasion beyond the standard header/footer CTAs.
- Other registers worth knowing: legal pages pair formal text with plain-English one-liners ("In other words, you might get sued."); free-tools pages open with the full usage recipe and state privacy as plain fact in one sentence (nothing leaves the reader's device); transparency dashboards are nearly copy-free — exact numbers do the persuasion.

## Rewrite instructions
When restyling a foreign document into buffer.com voice:

**Preserve from the source:** every fact, number, name, date, price, technical claim, and the document's language (do not translate). Keep the argument's structure of meaning — what is claimed, what supports it, what is concluded — and keep any legally or technically required statements verbatim. Keep exact figures exact; Buffer never rounds for comfort, so precision in the source is an asset, not a problem.

**Replace:** the voice, rhythm, lexicon, and formatting. Concretely:
- Recast to second person ("you") with "we" as the authoring team; kill passive voice and third-person institutional framing.
- Swap jargon for the preferred lexicon (see Lexicon): "leverage" → "use", "employees" → "teammates", "users" → "creators"/"people"/"customers", "solution" → the product's plain name.
- Break paragraphs to 1–3 sentences; add single-sentence pivot beats; convert buried benefits into "so you can …" tails and "without X-ing" relief clauses.
- Turn feature lists into verb triads; turn contrasts into mirrored antithesis ("X, not Y").
- Rewrite headings as sentence-case outcome promises; rewrite any Q&A so every answer opens with the verdict word.
- Strip urgency, hype superlatives, and fear framing entirely; if the source overclaims, hedge it the Buffer way ("tends to", "in this dataset") and volunteer the limitation with a workaround.
- Apply the formatting habits: numerals, spaced em-dashes, arrow UI paths, descriptive links, at most one functional emoji per callout, "X (Twitter)".
- If the piece sells anything, use the fixed CTA strings verbatim ("Get started for free"; closer "Grow your social presence with confidence" + "No credit card needed. Free forever.") — never invent new CTA copy.

**Imitate the synthetic samples strictly for register, rhythm, and structure — never copy their sentences.** They are cadence templates: match their sentence lengths, dash pivots, verdict-first answers, and parenthetical warmth with the source document's own content.

**Pick the sub-profile by target document type:** marketing/sales page → Landing; article, tutorial, or report → Blog/resources (add the named-"I" columnist, Key takeaways, and a de-hyped closing; use the data-study hedging register for anything statistics-driven); definition or concept explainer → Glossary; release note or product update → Changelog; investor or financial update → Shareholder letter; job posting or company-values page → Careers/culture; setup or configuration guide → Integrations how-to (drop the humor, keep numbered UI-verbatim steps). If nothing fits, default to the base voice with the Landing structure removed — short paragraphs, verdict-first candor, no CTA.
