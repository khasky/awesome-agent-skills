# Genre — the post into someone else's community

Covers every platform whose Genre column in `platforms.md` names this file; that table is the single list, and this file does not restate it. The defining fact: the audience is a community with its own moderators, its own rules, and its own memory — and a marketing post is a guest's behaviour there, judged as such.

## Human baseline

Somebody who belongs there posting something the room can use. It leads with the useful part, states the affiliation plainly, and survives the top comment. Register is flat and unadorned: these rooms read marketing cadence as an intrusion and downvote or remove it, often before a moderator sees it. Length matches the substance, and questions in the replies get answered by the author.

## Before writing — non-negotiable

Fetch and read the target's own rules (subreddit sidebar and wiki, community sidebar on `lemmy`, the group's pinned rules, the server's `#rules` channel, the site guidelines for `hackernews`), plus the last screen of what natives post there. A community that bans self-promotion, requires a participation ratio, mandates flair, or confines promo to one channel is a hard constraint: report it to the user and offer a different target rather than writing a post moderators will remove. `hackernews` additionally has its own form for a self-authored product (`Show HN:`), with its own expectations — a working thing and the author present in the comments.

## AI tells in this genre

| Tell | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Marketing cadence: a hook line, a benefit list, a closing CTA | Flat prose. State the thing, then the caveat |
| Disclosure buried or missing | One plain sentence: "I built this" / "we make this", where the reader meets the product |
| A post that answers nothing the community asked | Lead with the part usable by someone who never installs the product |
| Bold-lead bullets and heading stacks in a plain-text room | Match what the room actually posts; most of these surfaces are prose and code blocks |
| Praise-shaped opener ("Great community here!") | Delete |
| Symmetric "pros and cons" with no verdict | Say which one you would pick and why, including where yours loses |
| Claiming novelty the room can disprove in one comment | Name the alternatives honestly, including the ones that are better at something |
| Cross-posting the same text to two communities | Per-platform uniqueness is a publication requirement, and communities overlap by membership |
| A wall of links | One link, where it belongs — on the disclosure line for `reddit` |

## Rules

1. **The useful part comes first**, and it must stand alone: a reader who never clicks the link still got something.
2. **Disclose plainly**, in the post body, not in a comment added later.
3. **Title carries the post** on the aggregators — `reddit`, `lemmy`, `hackernews`, `daily-dev`. Editorializing titles sink submissions; the `hackernews` guidelines ban them outright, and a tag or flair the platform requires is part of submitting, not a detail.
4. **Flair, tags and required fields** are part of submitting — an unset required flair blocks the post.
5. **Answer the replies.** A campaign that posts into a community and leaves is the behaviour these rules exist against; say so to the user when the schedule assumes nobody is home.
6. **Frequency is per community**, and low. Several posts a week into one room is the pattern moderators ban for, whatever the campaign's global frequency says.
7. **Name what a tool is the first time it appears.** These rooms are mixed: a reader who knows the ecosystem and a reader who arrived from the front page are both there. A paragraph that compares products by bare name — "Agent Teams does not change this. MCP Agent Mail gets closest with file reservations" — loses the second reader in one line. First mention carries a short identifying clause: `Agent Teams (Claude Code's experimental multi-agent mode)`, `MCP Agent Mail (a third-party coordination server for AI coding agents)`. It costs six words and it is the difference between a post the room can use and a post only insiders can parse.
8. **Flat register is not a lifeless one.** These rooms punish marketing cadence, not personality. The post still has a person behind it: a question the room is already asking, an aside about what surprised you, one plain admission that something did not work. What flat rules out is the hook-benefit-CTA shape, not the author. A post with no human signal anywhere reads as generated, which these rooms spot faster than most.
9. **Plain spoken English**, same bar as the other genres: no `duller`, `says it outright`, `thus`, `myriad`. Technical precision stays; literary vocabulary goes.
