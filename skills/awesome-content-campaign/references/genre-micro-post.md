# Genre — the short feed post

Covers every platform whose Genre column in `platforms.md` names this file: the ones whose native unit is a short post in a scrolling feed. That table and the Phase 3 live check own the structural limits; this file is about register. It never restates which platforms those are — the table is the single list.

## Human baseline

One idea, landed fast. A person posting on their own feed opens where the interest is, says the thing, and stops — no setup paragraph, no summary of what was just said. Length is set by the idea, so a real feed is uneven: a three-line post sits next to a twelve-line one. Specifics do the persuading: a version, a number with its condition, a name, the actual error text. First person and contractions are normal. The reader is assumed competent.

## Read the feed first

Before writing for a platform, read a handful of recent posts that natives — not brands — publish there, and match their length norms, punctuation habits and formatting. Half of what reads as machine-written is register mismatch rather than word choice. When a voice profile from `awesome-content-voice` is in play it outranks this observation for the author's own habits; the feed still sets what the platform's readers expect.

## AI tells in this genre

| Tell | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| The topic-survey opener: "In today's fast-moving world of X…", or a definition of the thing every reader already knows | Open at the concrete moment: the bug, the number, the decision, the thing someone said |
| Bold-lead bullet stack where three sentences would do | Prose. Lists only for things that are genuinely a list |
| Every post the same length and shape — same hook line, same body, same closing CTA | Vary length and shape across the batch; a short post is allowed to be short |
| Rule of three everywhere: three adjectives, three benefits, three parallel clauses | Two or four. Break the parallel |
| Negative parallelism: "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." / "It's not about X, it's about Y" | Say the one thing meant |
| Emoji as bullet points or decoration | Per the campaign's emoji setting, and only where the platform's natives use them. Placed after the phrase that earned the reaction, reporting what it was: 🤯 on the number nobody expected, 🧐 on the caveat |
| A URL alone between two paragraphs | The link goes on the same line as the sentence pointing at it: `…turns a video model into a channel. Read more: <url>` |
| A trailing line of bare tag words (`ai machine-learning video news`) | Tags with their own composer field live in frontmatter; only platforms whose natives write inline `#hashtags` get them in the body |
| A phrase shaped like a saying with no saying behind it ("proved it the blunt way") | Ordinary words for what happened: "someone at fal just tried it" |
| Hype closer: "the future is bright", "excited to see where this goes" | End at the content, or on the open question |
| A benefit with no mechanism: "makes your workflow seamless" | The mechanism or the number: what it does, how much faster, under what conditions |
| The same closing CTA line stamped on every post of the batch | Vary the wording and the placement; the link goes where interest peaks |
| Perfect uniform sentence rhythm | Let one sentence run long and the next be four words |

## Rules

1. **One idea per post.** A post that needs a second idea is two posts or a thread.
2. **The first line is the whole hook.** On `linkedin` and `facebook-wall` the feed cuts at a "see more" fold, so the point cannot live below it; on `x` and `bluesky` the cap does the same job.
3. **Concrete over categorical.** One real number with its condition beats three adjectives. Numbers come from the knowledge map, with the map's exact values.
4. **Link discipline** — exactly one primary link, placed where the reader's interest peaks, never opening the post and never twice; `instagram` and `tiktok` captions do not link, so the CTA is bio phrasing and the URL still travels in frontmatter.
5. **Threads only when the content is genuinely sequential**, and only where the post file says so — a thread is a structure, not a way past the character cap.
6. **Hashtags per the researched norm**: a few where they are the discovery mechanism (`mastodon`, `instagram`, `tumblr` tags), none where they read as noise. Where the composer has a tag field, the tags go there and never into the prose.
7. **Emoji budget: 0 to 5 for a whole text, scaled to length** — none or one in a micro-post, one or two in a medium one. Spacing carries more than count: two in the same paragraph, or a pair a line apart, reads as decoration however apt each one is.
8. **Leave room.** One plain sentence, one ordinary observation. A post where every line is engineered to land reads engineered.
