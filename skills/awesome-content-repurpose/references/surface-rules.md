# Surface rules - the smaller rules that support Phase 5 to 7

`SKILL.md` keeps the rules a reader's choice between versions turned on. These are the rules that support them, one construction each. They bind in the same way, they are read with Phase 5, and the audit in Phase 9 counts them under `Style` and reads them under `Fidelity`.

Where a rule names a guide's furniture (paths, clients, setup blocks, the routing split), it applies to a guide source. An essay, a benchmark or a retrospective takes the rule's shape with its own modules in the slots, and a rule with no slot for the source in hand is skipped rather than forced.

- **Numbers are written at the precision they were measured, with no decoration zeros.** `1.00%` is `1%`, `635.0` is `635`, and a value the source states loosely (`about 1%`) keeps the source's own precision.
- **Hedges travel with their numbers.** `about a dozen`, `roughly fifty`, `the most common shape` and `about a third` keep their qualifiers in every form. The short form is where a hedge is most often dropped for room, and a sentence that needs the room drops a clause instead.
- **Populations stay what they are.** A count of repositories is not a count of templates, a queried set is not the answered set, and a named list of examples is not a total. Say which population a number belongs to whenever two are in play.
- Fragments are a level decision: allowed outside the short form at level 1, ordinary at 2 and 3. A fragment lands because the sentence before it earned the pause.
- **On a guide, every opener names what stays and what changes, and says the change is possible rather than done.** Its first sentence carries the subject's name and at least one client's product name. A first sentence built from category nouns alone fails, however elegant.
- **Both primary paths survive into every form, in the title and in the body.** A title carries both clients or one collective noun, and every body names both at least once. Dropping the second client from the short form is not compression. It is a different post.
- Path sections keep the source's order in the long form. A secondary tool never stands in a title beside the subject.
- No meta-framing anywhere: no sentence about how the topic is usually framed, argued or decided. The forms explain the material and say what the author would do with it.
- No self-positioning contrast in an opener (`I read <subject> as X rather than Y`). The stance is stated forward, and `rather than` earns its place later, inside a sentence about the work.
- Never point at the form's own blocks with a demonstrative, never count or rank what is about to appear, never point at a position inside a block. Name what it holds or state it in words.
- No self-benefit framing. The judgment goes on the subject, and first person stays on what the author would do.
- Plain spoken US English, in the order a person says it: somebody does something to something. No idiom outside ordinary speech, and no sentence that needs a second read to parse.
- **One question to the reader per form at most, and none in a family whose writers do not ask** (`references/registers.md`). A question-shaped section heading is a label and does not count. Every question is one the post answers or the reader can answer from their own setup: one clause, one thing asked, every noun in it found in the body, nothing invented to make it concrete.
- **In the short form every sentence does one job**: for a guide the promise, the proof, the split, the caveat; for anything else the scene, the turn, the finding, the number, the verdict. A sentence that states an abstract property without telling the reader what to do with it is cut.
- **Every line of the short form is a complete sentence with a verb**, except the turn after the scene, which may be a fragment of at most 6 words. A label with no verb (`<A>/<B>, add <subject>`) asserts nothing.
- **A form points at nothing it does not carry.** Where the form has no block, naming the variables, the key or the script sends the reader to hunt for them. The sentence those words would have filled carries the promise, the proof or the caveat instead.
- **Every sentence makes one claim the reader can check.** A sentence that gathers several findings and closes on a collective verdict (`it all points the same way`) asserts a relation instead of showing one.
- No inventory of the steps as a summary line (`Three variables for <A>, one script for <B>`). The steps are shown once, in their blocks, and a lead line states the promise instead.
- Inside a path section only the block is the anchor. The sentences around it are frames, written for that form.
- No two consecutive paragraphs open on the same words, compared on the whole opening phrase.
- **Claims about effort, cost and what is expensive to replace come from the source or are not written.** A list of what is costly to rebuild that includes something the reader changes with one flag reads as invented, and one invented item takes the rest of the sentence with it.
- No self-praise and no caption of the obvious. A sentence saying the post, the setup or the idea is simple, useful or the entire idea is deleted. A sentence whose subject is the thought itself (`The useful idea here is that ...`) is cut back to the thought: delete everything up to and including `that`, and if the rest stands, that was the fix.
- **A regular heading reads without its markers.** On a plain-text platform the publisher replaces `## ` with an emoji, so a heading is a short line of plain words that names what the section carries, never a lone symbol, a trailing colon or a question that only works as a label.
- **Commands and dotted tokens are code spans.** Every command line lives in a fenced block in the long form, every inline flag, path, env var and dotted token (`CLAUDE.md`, `api.example.com`) is a code span, and nothing shaped like a URL is left bare except the link line. Platforms that autolink rewrite what they find, and the file is the only place that can be prevented.
- **A comparison never depends on column alignment outside a fenced block.** Runs of spaces collapse in every plain composer.
- **Two tildes in one block read as strikethrough** in rich editors that flatten blocks. Write `about 7-17x` rather than an approximation sign, or keep the two values in separate blocks.
- No calendar date in prose except an `as of` qualifier on a correction of a temporary state.
