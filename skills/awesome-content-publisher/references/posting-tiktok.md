# tiktok

Check: `tiktok.com/tiktokstudio/upload?tab=photo` signed in shows `Select photos to upload`; signed out shows the login wall.

Compose: the `Photos` tab (`tab "Photos"`) takes up to 35 photos per post, 50 MB each, JPG, JPEG, PNG or WebP, through `Select photos` (an OS file chooser, answered with `browser_file_upload`) or drag and drop. The caption field, its counter, the hashtag suggestions, the cover picker and `Who can view this post` render only after the photos are in, so the first run reads them from the live form and records the caption cap in the platform cache. The caption carries the frontmatter `title` and the tag line, nothing else; the body goes into the first comment after publishing (below). `Post` submits; the studio's `Posts` list is the read-back.

Read-back: `tiktok.com/@<handle>`, newest photo post; confirm the caption and the photo count.

**What goes where (user's rule, 2026-10-01).** Three places, each with one job:

- the title field (`0/90`): the frontmatter `title`, the card's headline in the feed;
- the caption (`0/4000`, labelled `Описание` / `Description`): the frontmatter `title` again, a blank line, then the committed hashtags. The body is never typed here;
- the first comment, posted by this account on the published post: the body, link line included, exactly as the form file carries it, with its paragraphs joined by single spaces (the box holds no line breaks). The comment box caps at 2200 (counter `N/2200`); a body over that is a Phase 3b question, not a cut at the composer.

Comment route: open the photo permalink `tiktok.com/@<handle>/photo/<id>`, select the `Комментарии` / `Comments` tab of the side panel, click `[data-e2e="comment-text"]` (a Draft.js box), `insertText` the body, and submit with `[data-e2e="comment-post"]`, the pink arrow. The comment holds no line breaks: `Enter` and `Shift+Enter` both submit whatever is in the box, so one run posted the first paragraph alone as a stray comment. Join the paragraphs and the link line with single spaces and insert the whole body in one call. A URL in a comment stays plain text, like one in a caption. Read back that the comment shows under the post with the account's name and the full text, link included. A comment is part of this platform's publish: the ledger entry stays `in_progress` until it is read back, and a post without its comment is `degraded`.

Editing a published post: TikTok Studio `Публикации` / `Posts` (`tiktok.com/tiktokstudio/content`) gives each row a pencil that opens `tiktokstudio/upload/post/photo?item_id=<id>`, where the title, the caption and the settings are editable and `Сохранить` / `Save` applies them. So a caption that went out wrong is fixed in place, never by a repost — within 7 days of publishing. After that the whole form is greyed out (`[class*=disabledMask]` over every field, measured on posts 8 and 10 days old), and the post keeps what it shipped with. The form takes mouse clicks at coordinates only (Playwright's `click` waits forever on its stability check), commits a tag reliably only with about 3 seconds between typing `#name` and `Enter`, and raises a `beforeunload` dialog on every navigation away except `Сохранить`. One run's caption published empty (`Нет описания` in the list, `0/4000` in the edit form) while the title field kept its text: read the caption back from the Studio row, not only from the title shown on the card.

A hashtag typed as text is not a hashtag here. TikTok's caption field only links a tag that was committed through its own suggestion list: type `#` and the tag's letters, wait for the dropdown, then click the entry you want. A committed tag renders **bold** in the caption box, and on the published post it is a clickable link; a tag that was merely typed stays plain text in both places and indexes nothing. One run pasted four tags as part of the caption string and shipped four dead words.

So the caption goes in in two moves: insert the title and a blank line, then add the tags one at a time — type `#` plus the name, wait about a second for the suggestion list, click the matching entry, confirm the tag turned bold, and repeat. Where a name has no suggestion, TikTok still offers it as a new tag at the top of the list; clicking that entry commits it the same way. Before submitting, count the bold tags against the frontmatter `hashtags`; after publishing, confirm each one is an anchor on the post page.

The caption's `0/90` counter belongs to a separate title field, not to the caption, whose cap is 4000. Read the counter that sits under the caption box, never the first one on the page.

Verified on a live photo post, with the tag route simpler than the paragraph above implies: the suggestion list did not answer a selector probe at all, and `Enter` after the typed `#name` committed the tag anyway — each one rendered bold in the caption and published as a `/tag/` anchor. So type `#` plus the name, wait a second, press `Enter`, type a space, and count the bold tags before submitting; click the list only where `Enter` leaves a plain word. The title field above the caption reads `Add a catchy title` with the `0/90` counter and takes a coordinate click plus typing; it is the card's headline in the feed, so it gets the post's title. `Post` lands on `tiktok.com/tiktokstudio/content`, where the new row reads `Content under review` with privacy `Only me` for the first minutes — that is the platform's review hold, not a failed publish. The row's anchor is a `/video/<id>` link that redirects to `/@<handle>/photo/<id>`; record the photo URL, and read the caption, the tag anchors and the photo count there. The permalink rendered publicly during the review hold on this run, but say in the ledger that the hold was showing when the post was recorded.
